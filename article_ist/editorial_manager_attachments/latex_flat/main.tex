\begin{abstract}
\textbf{Context.}
Software processes generate event data that process mining (PM) and
stochastic models can use for forecasting, yet their integration for
software development lifecycle (SDLC) prediction is reported only
anecdotally.
\textbf{Objective.}
To map the state of the art at the intersection of process mining,
stochastic modeling, and machine-learning forecasting applied to software
processes; to identify research gaps; and to propose a research agenda.
\textbf{Method.}
We executed a systematic mapping study reported against
SEGRESS~\cite{kitchenham2023segress}, with LLM-assisted screening
(\textit{claude-haiku-4-5} as primary screener;
\textit{claude-sonnet-4-6} as verifier). Searches were executed on
12~April~2026 over January~1994--December~2025. From 5{,}783 deduplicated
records plus 364 control items, two corpus tiers were screened: 2{,}340
working-set and 3{,}807 auxiliary papers (6{,}147 in total). Cohen's
$\kappa$ was computed on stratified 20\% LLM--LLM samples and on an
independent blind human sample ($n=468$ T/A, $n=177$ FT). An 8-criterion
reporting-quality checklist was applied to all confirmed includes.
\textbf{Results.}
340 distinct primary studies were confirmed after cross-tier deduplication
(404 raw confirmations: 169 working-set + 212 + 23 auxiliary). LLM--LLM
agreement was substantial ($\kappa_{\text{binary}} = 0.695$ T/A,
$0.694$ FT); human--LLM agreement was fair at T/A ($0.335$) and slight at
FT ($0.122$), with 75\% Lost Evidence at full-text screening. Findings:
F1 descriptive ceiling; F2 ad-hoc event-log construction; F3 weak
stochastic--PM integration; F4 pipeline fragmentation (only 1 of 340
papers jointly satisfies PM, stochastic modeling, and forecasting);
F5 process-derived ML features unexplored.
\textbf{Conclusion.}
The literature is descriptively rich but predictively fragmented. We propose
the SPMF taxonomy and a four-direction research agenda. PATHCAST is sketched
as a future-work hypothesis, not as an empirically validated pipeline.
\textbf{Limitations.}
Structural scarcity claims depend on an LLM selection protocol whose recall
against a human gold standard remains limited at full-text screening;
deduplication reduced the combined corpus from 404 to 340 studies.
\end{abstract}

\section*{Declaration of generative AI in the writing process}
During the conduct of this systematic literature review, the authors used
\textit{claude-haiku-4-5-20251001} (Anthropic) as the primary LLM screener
at the title/abstract and full-text stages and as the LLM scorer for the
quality-assessment rubric, and \textit{claude-sonnet-4-6} (Anthropic) as
the cross-model verifier for inter-rater agreement. All decisions were
inspected by the authors; the LLM tools are not listed as authors and
bear no responsibility for editorial judgements. Full prompts, JSON
schemas, and per-paper decisions are deposited in the replication package
(\href{\zenododoiurl}{Zenodo
DOI~\zenododoi}). The authors reviewed and edited the content
as needed and take full responsibility for the content of the
publication.

\section*{Data availability}
