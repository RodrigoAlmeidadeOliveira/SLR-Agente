\begin{abstract}
Software development organizations generate large volumes of process data
through \textit{issue} management systems, version control repositories,
continuous integration (CI/CD) pipelines, and code review platforms.
Despite this instrumentation, delivery forecasting still relies largely on
aggregated metrics and on throughput-based Monte Carlo simulation that
treat the development workflow as a black box: they observe \emph{how many}
items finish per period and ignore \emph{how} work traverses states,
queues, and rework.
The operational pain that motivates this thesis is therefore
\textbf{process-unaware delivery forecasting under uncertainty}---forecasts
that neither respect process structure nor produce actionable
distributions (for example P50/P85/P95).

This thesis \textbf{proposes} PATHCAST (PATH-aware foreCASTing), a
computational method for the probabilistic forecasting of software
development processes, based on the integration of process mining,
stochastic modeling through Markov chains, Monte Carlo simulation, and
machine learning.
The method transforms heterogeneous data from software development process
repositories into structured event logs, discovers process models from
those logs, and derives state-transition models that enable simulation of
execution trajectories and of
\textbf{outcome distributions}---here understood as distributions of
execution time, of path through the flow, and of delivery-related outcomes
under uncertainty.
The research follows the \textit{Design Science Research} methodology.
The empirical evaluation compares PATHCAST with baselines based on
traditional repository metrics and on throughput-based Monte Carlo.
The expected result is that explicit incorporation of process structure
improves predictive accuracy and interpretability, advancing process
intelligence in software engineering.
	\keywords{Process Mining, Probabilistic Forecasting, Markov Chains, Monte Carlo Simulation, Machine Learning, Design Science Research}
\end{abstract}
