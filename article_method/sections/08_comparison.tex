\section{Theoretical Comparison with Related Approaches}
\label{sec:comparison}

We compare PATHCAST with four families of related approaches along
five theoretical dimensions: the process model assumed or discovered,
the unit at which stochastic sampling operates, the explicit modelling
of rework, the capacity to represent state-specific delays, and the
form of the forecast output. Table~\ref{tab:approach-comparison}
summarises the comparison; the paragraphs that follow discuss each
family.

\paragraph{Throughput Monte Carlo.}
The state-of-practice approach in agile
forecasting~\cite{vacanti2015, savage2009flaw, magennis2015forecasting}
samples future cumulative completion from the empirical throughput
distribution of a team. It is operationally simple and widely adopted,
but treats the process as a black box: the trajectory $\psi$ is
collapsed, so neither rework nor state-specific delays are
representable. By Theorem~\ref{thm:information}, throughput-based
Monte Carlo cannot exploit the information gain
$I(T;\psi) > 0$ encoded in the trajectory; PATHCAST does, by sampling
$T^{(m)}$ jointly with $\psi^{(m)}$ in Algorithm~\ref{alg:mc-full}.

\paragraph{Analytical absorbing Markov chains.}
The classical closed-form expressions
$N\mathbf{1}$ and $NR$ for absorbing
chains~\cite{kemeny1976finite, Spedicato2017} yield expected absorption
times and absorption probabilities, but no forecast distribution. They
are deterministic in the parameters and silent about quantiles or tail
risk. PATHCAST uses these expressions for analytical cross-validation
(Section~\ref{sec:numerical-example}) but produces distributional
forecasts via Stage~4, recovering the percentiles
$\hat{Q}_\alpha$ that operational forecasting
requires~\cite{vacanti2015}.

\paragraph{Non-Markovian stochastic Petri nets.}
NM-SPN~\cite{RoggeSolti2015NonMarkovianSPN} parameterise timed firings
with general distributions over an exogenously specified Petri net.
PATHCAST differs in two respects.
\emph{First,} the process model is discovered from the event log by
Stage~2 rather than supplied externally, eliminating the
domain-expert modelling step that NM-SPN requires.
\emph{Second,} sojourn distributions are empirical rather than
parametric, avoiding misspecification of the heavy-tailed,
zero-inflated distributions typical of software process
sojourns (Remark~\ref{rem:nonparametric}). NM-SPN's expressivity ---
in particular, race policies and concurrent firings --- exceeds
PATHCAST's first-order Markov assumption (A1 in
Table~\ref{tab:assumptions}); PATHCAST trades that expressivity for
estimation from data without expert modelling.

\paragraph{Predictive process monitoring and lead-time ML.}
The predictive process-monitoring
literature~\cite{polato2018time, verenich2019survey} and the
effort-estimation strand of software
engineering~\cite{choetkiertikul2018deep} address remaining-time
prediction via supervised learning over case-level features. PATHCAST
occupies a complementary niche: it integrates a stochastic process
model (not a black-box predictor) and is specialised to the
heterogeneous data sources of the SDLC (Git, issue tracker, CI/CD,
code review). The ML component (Section~\ref{sec:ml-component}) bridges
to this literature by adding case-level point forecasts on top of the
population-level distribution, with features 1--8 of
Table~\ref{tab:features} exposing the PM- and Markov-derived signals
that supervised baselines do not see.
