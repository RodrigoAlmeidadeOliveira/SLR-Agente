\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

% ---------- §2.1 Process mining fundamentals ----------
\subsection{Process Mining}
\label{sec:bg-pm}

Process mining~\cite{aalst2016process} extracts process knowledge from
event logs --- sequences of timestamped activity occurrences grouped
by case identifier --- through three families of techniques:
\emph{discovery} (inferring a model from a log),
\emph{conformance checking} (comparing observed traces to a reference
model via alignments), and \emph{enhancement} (enriching a model with
frequency or performance information). PATHCAST adopts the Inductive
Miner~\cite{leemans2013discovering} for discovery, motivated by its
soundness guarantee on the resulting process tree
(Property~\ref{prop:soundness}), which is required by the contract of
Stage~3 (Table~\ref{tab:contracts}). The standard yardstick for
discovered models is the four-quality
framework~\cite{aalst2016process} --- fitness, precision,
generalisation, and simplicity. PATHCAST evaluates the first three
explicitly (Equation~\ref{eq:conformance}); simplicity is bounded by
construction through the SDLC activity taxonomy of Stage~1.

% ---------- §2.2 Markov chains for business processes ----------
\subsection{Absorbing Markov Chains}
\label{sec:bg-markov}

Absorbing Markov chains provide closed-form expressions for the
expected number of visits to each transient state and the absorption
probability into each terminal state, via the fundamental matrix
$N = (I - Q)^{-1}$ and the absorption matrix
$B = NR$~\cite{kemeny1976finite}. Their applicability rests on a
first-order Markov assumption and on the certainty of absorption from
every transient state (Property~\ref{prop:absorption-certainty}).
Estimation of $P$ from event-log counts is a standard MLE
problem~\cite{Spedicato2017, billingsley1961statistical}, with the
sample complexity governed by the row-wise observation counts.
Rogge-Solti and Weske~\cite{RoggeSolti2015NonMarkovianSPN} extended
this line of work to non-Markovian stochastic Petri nets for general
business processes, parameterising firings with arbitrary
distributions over an exogenously specified Petri net. PATHCAST stays
within the absorbing-chain framework --- preserving the closed-form
analytical results --- but couples the chain with empirical
sojourn-time distributions sampled inside Monte Carlo, so that the
parametric assumptions of classical semi-Markov treatments are not
required.

% ---------- §2.3 Stochastic forecasting in software engineering ----------
\subsection{Stochastic Forecasting in Software Engineering}
\label{sec:bg-forecasting}

Throughput-based Monte
Carlo~\cite{vacanti2015, savage2009flaw, magennis2015forecasting} is
the state-of-practice approach to lead-time forecasting in agile
software development. It samples future cumulative completion from the
empirical throughput distribution of a team --- typically the number
of items completed per day over a recent window --- and propagates the
sample forward to estimate the probability of meeting a deadline. The
method is operationally simple and process-agnostic; its limitation is
the collapse of the trajectory, which precludes the modelling of
rework or state-specific delays. Adjacent literature includes
time-series and sequence models for business
processes~\cite{polato2018time}, supervised remaining-time
prediction~\cite{verenich2019survey}, and deep-learning effort
estimation from issue-tracker
features~\cite{choetkiertikul2018deep}. None of these is specialised
to the SDLC or integrates the heterogeneous data sources that software
repositories provide.

% ---------- §2.4 Gap synthesis ----------
\subsection{Pipeline-Fragmentation Gap}
\label{sec:bg-gap}

Our companion systematic literature review~\cite{almeida2026slr}
screened 5{,}783 deduplicated records and confirmed 404 primary
studies at the intersection of process mining, stochastic modelling,
and forecasting for software processes. The review reports five
findings; the one that motivates the present paper is finding F4 ---
\emph{pipeline fragmentation}: only a single primary study in the
corpus jointly satisfies the three dimensions. The remaining studies
sit in one of three partial regions: PM without forecasting,
forecasting without process awareness, or stochastic modelling without
integration with the heterogeneous data sources of software
repositories. Table~\ref{tab:gap-synthesis} summarises this
fragmentation across five representative families.
