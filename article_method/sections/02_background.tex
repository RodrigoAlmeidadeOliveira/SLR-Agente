\section{Background and Related Work}
\label{sec:background}

% ---------- §2.1 Process mining fundamentals ----------
\subsection{Process Mining}
\label{sec:bg-pm}

Process mining~\cite{aalst2016process} extracts process knowledge from
event logs --- sequences of timestamped activity occurrences grouped by
case identifier. The discipline distinguishes three families of
techniques: \emph{discovery}, which infers a process model from an
event log; \emph{conformance checking}, which compares observed traces
to a reference model via alignments; and \emph{enhancement}, which
enriches a model with frequency or performance information.
PATHCAST uses the Inductive Miner~\cite{leemans2013discovering} as its
discovery algorithm, motivated by its soundness guarantee on the
discovered process tree (Property~\ref{prop:soundness}), a property
required by the contract of Stage~3 (Table~\ref{tab:contracts}). The
\emph{four-quality framework}~\cite{aalst2016process} ---
fitness, precision, generalisation, and simplicity --- is the standard
yardstick for discovered models; PATHCAST adopts the first three
explicitly (Equation~\ref{eq:conformance}) and constrains simplicity by
construction through the bounded SDLC activity taxonomy of Stage~1.

% ---------- §2.2 Markov chains for business processes ----------
\subsection{Absorbing Markov Chains}
\label{sec:bg-markov}

Absorbing Markov chains provide closed-form expressions for the
expected number of visits to each transient state and the absorption
probability into each terminal state, via the fundamental matrix
$N = (I - Q)^{-1}$ and the absorption matrix
$B = NR$~\cite{kemeny1976finite}. Their applicability rests on a
first-order Markov assumption and on the certainty of absorption from
every transient state (Property~\ref{prop:absorption-certainty}).
Estimation of $P$ from event-log counts is a standard MLE
problem~\cite{Spedicato2017, billingsley1961statistical}, with the
sample complexity governed by the row-wise observation counts.
Rogge-Solti and Weske~\cite{RoggeSolti2015NonMarkovianSPN} extended
this line of work to non-Markovian stochastic Petri nets for general
business processes, parameterising firings with arbitrary
distributions over an exogenously specified Petri net. PATHCAST stays
within the absorbing-chain framework --- preserving the closed-form
analytical results --- but couples the chain with empirical
sojourn-time distributions sampled inside Monte Carlo, so that the
parametric assumptions of classical semi-Markov treatments are not
required.

% ---------- §2.3 Stochastic forecasting in software engineering ----------
\subsection{Stochastic Forecasting in Software Engineering}
\label{sec:bg-forecasting}

The state-of-practice approach to lead-time forecasting in agile
software development is throughput-based Monte
Carlo~\cite{vacanti2015, savage2009flaw, magennis2015forecasting}.
The method samples future cumulative completion from the empirical
throughput distribution of a team --- typically the number of items
completed per day over a recent window --- and propagates the sample
forward to estimate the probability of meeting a deadline. It is
operationally simple, makes no assumption about the process structure,
and has been widely adopted in agile practice; its limitation is the
collapse of the trajectory, which prevents the modelling of rework or
state-specific delays. Recent work on probabilistic process
forecasting in business processes
includes time-series and sequence
models~\cite{polato2018time} and remaining-time prediction via
supervised learning~\cite{verenich2019survey}; in the
effort-estimation strand of software
engineering, deep-learning approaches operate on case-level
features extracted from issue
trackers~\cite{choetkiertikul2018deep}. None of these methods is
specialised to the SDLC nor integrates the heterogeneous data sources
that software repositories provide.

% ---------- §2.4 Gap synthesis ----------
\subsection{Pipeline-Fragmentation Gap}
\label{sec:bg-gap}

The systematic literature review of~\cite{almeida2026slr} screened
5{,}783 deduplicated records and confirmed 404 primary studies at the
intersection of process mining, stochastic modelling, and forecasting
for software processes. The review reports five findings; the one
that motivates the present paper is finding F4 ---
\emph{pipeline fragmentation}: only a single primary study in the
corpus jointly satisfies the three dimensions PM, stochastic
modelling, and forecasting. The remaining studies sit in one of three
partial regions: PM without forecasting, forecasting without process
awareness, or stochastic modelling without integration with the
heterogeneous data sources of software repositories.
Table~\ref{tab:gap-synthesis} summarises this fragmentation across
five representative families. PATHCAST is positioned at the
intersection of all three dimensions; the formal pipeline of
Section~\ref{sec:architecture} and the properties of
Section~\ref{sec:properties} are its technical instantiation.
