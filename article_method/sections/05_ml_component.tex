\section{Machine-Learning Enhancement Component}
\label{sec:ml-component}

The ML component $\mathcal{S}_{\textup{ML}}$ runs in parallel to the
sequential pipeline (Figure~\ref{fig:pipeline-architecture}) and
produces a case-level predictor $\hat{f}$ and feature-importance
vector $\mathbf{w}$. Its contract is the last row of
Table~\ref{tab:contracts}: input $(\mathcal{L}, \Phi, P, N)$, output
$(\hat{f}, \mathbf{w})$, with postconditions on temporal hold-out
enforcement and on the set of reported metrics. The novelty is the
feature family: half of the features are derived from the PM and
Markov stages and are unavailable to repository-mining baselines.

The companion empirical paper evaluates three feature sets:
\textbf{PM-only} (features 1--8), \textbf{MSR-only} (features 9--15),
and \textbf{Combined} (features 1--15). Features 6 and 7 instantiate
Theorem~\ref{thm:information}: they inject the
trajectory-conditional expectations $\mathbb{E}[T \mid \psi]$ and
$P(\textup{Done} \mid \psi)$ directly into the predictor --- quantities
that are by construction inaccessible to MSR-only baselines.

% ---------- §5.2 Label construction ----------
\subsection{Label Construction}
\label{sec:ml-labels}

\begin{definition}[Outcome Label]
\label{def:label}
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:label}
y_c \;=\;
\begin{cases}
1\;\;\textup{(\texttt{delayed})}  & \textup{if } T_c > \tau_{\textup{threshold}}, \\
0\;\;\textup{(\texttt{on\_time})} & \textup{otherwise}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

Three threshold strategies are supported and reported separately:
\emph{(i)~percentile-based}, $\tau_{\textup{threshold}} = \hat{Q}_{0.75}$
of the training-set lead-time distribution;
\emph{(ii)~SLA-based}, a fixed organisational deadline;
\emph{(iii)~relative}, $\tau_{\textup{threshold}} =
2 \cdot \tilde{d}_{\textup{type}}$, twice the median lead time of the
issue type. The label definition is decoupled from the predictor, so
that any of the three thresholds can be selected at deployment.

% ---------- §5.3 Training protocol ----------
\subsection{Training Protocol}
\label{sec:ml-training}

The protocol enforces strict temporal separation between train and
test data.

\begin{algorithm}[ht]
\caption{ML Training with Temporal Hold-Out}
\label{alg:ml-training}
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\REQUIRE Event log $\mathcal{L}$, cutoff $t_{\textup{cut}}$,
         feature extractor $\textsc{Featurize}$,
         candidate model family
         $\mathcal{F} = \{\textup{RF}, \textup{XGBoost},
                          \textup{LogReg}\}$
\ENSURE  Trained model $\hat{f}$, test metrics $\mathcal{M}$,
         feature importances $\mathbf{w}$
\STATE   $\mathcal{L}_{\textup{train}} \gets
         \{c : \max(\tau(\sigma_c)) \le t_{\textup{cut}}\}$
\STATE   $\mathcal{L}_{\textup{test}}  \gets
         \{c : \min(\tau(\sigma_c)) > t_{\textup{cut}}\}$
\STATE   $X_{\textup{train}}, y_{\textup{train}} \gets
         \textsc{Featurize}(\mathcal{L}_{\textup{train}})$
\STATE   $X_{\textup{test}},  y_{\textup{test}}  \gets
         \textsc{Featurize}(\mathcal{L}_{\textup{test}})$
\FOR{each candidate $f_k \in \mathcal{F}$}
    \STATE $\textup{score}_k \gets
           \textsc{TemporalCV}(f_k, X_{\textup{train}},
                               y_{\textup{train}}, k=5)$
\ENDFOR
\STATE $\hat{f} \gets \arg\max_k \textup{score}_k$
\STATE $\hat{y}_{\textup{test}} \gets \hat{f}(X_{\textup{test}})$
\STATE $\mathcal{M} \gets
       \{\textup{AUC-ROC}, \textup{F1},
         \textup{Brier}, \textup{MAE}, \textup{MAPE}\}$
\STATE $\mathbf{w} \gets \textsc{PermutationImportance}(\hat{f},
       X_{\textup{test}}, y_{\textup{test}})$
\RETURN $\hat{f}$, $\mathcal{M}$, $\mathbf{w}$
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

The inner cross-validation $\textsc{TemporalCV}$ uses expanding
windows: fold $k$ trains on $[1, t_k]$ and validates on
$[t_k, t_{k+1}]$, in line with the recommendations
of~\cite{tashman2000out, hyndman2021forecasting}. The split rule
$\max(\tau(\sigma_c)) \le t_{\textup{cut}}$ for training and
$\min(\tau(\sigma_c)) > t_{\textup{cut}}$ for testing rules out any
case that straddles the cutoff, eliminating temporal information
leakage.

% ---------- §5.4 Integration with Monte Carlo ----------
\subsection{Integration with Monte Carlo Forecasts}
\label{sec:ml-integration}

Stage~4 (Section~\ref{sec:stages-s4}) produces
\emph{population-level distributional} forecasts; the ML component
produces \emph{case-level point} forecasts. The two are linked by a
deviation score that flags cases departing from the population
baseline.

\begin{definition}[Deviation Score]
\label{def:deviation}
For a case $c$ currently in state $s = \mu(e_{\textup{last}})$,
\begin{equation}
\label{eq:deviation}
\delta_c \;=\;
   \hat{f}(\mathbf{x}_c) \;-\;
   \hat{P}_{\textup{MC}}(\textup{delayed} \mid s_0 = s),
\end{equation}
where $\hat{P}_{\textup{MC}}(\textup{delayed} \mid s_0 = s)$ is the
Monte Carlo estimate of the delay probability for a population case
starting in state $s$.
\end{definition}

By construction $\delta_c > 0$ indicates above-baseline delay risk and
$\delta_c < 0$ indicates below-baseline risk, providing operationally
actionable case ranking on top of the population forecast.
